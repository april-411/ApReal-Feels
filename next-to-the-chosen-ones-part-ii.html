The Wind, The Fence & The Energy Exchange

The wife of wisdom had learned that silence could be a language of its own.

The new neighbors settled into their home, and the couple in the blue house continued tending to the life they had built. They watered their garden. They cared for their daughters. They worked, rested, prayed, and returned to the quiet rituals that kept their sacred space sacred.

But the wind had begun to carry more than leaves.

There were whispers—little echoes that traveled through the tree line. Similarities. Coincidences. Words that seemed to arrive from beyond the boundary of their own conversations. The wife noticed. She did not announce what she noticed. She grew quiet and watched.

Her husband, however, was a man of warmth. He waved to the neighbor. He called out a greeting. He extended invitations—the kind that came naturally to a man who believed a good meal, a cigar, and conversation could make a bridge between strangers.

The wife offered pleasant smiles, too. But her body was teaching her something her manners had not yet said aloud.

She returned to the somatic teachings she had studied: listen beyond the words. Notice what happens in the body when a presence enters the room. Pay attention to the tightening, the pause, the breath that changes before the mind can explain why.

She listened to the wind.

And she remembered a story about a frog who believed his well was the whole world.

He believed his well was the entire world. He boasted of his territory, his water, his freedom, and how fortunate anyone would be to live as he did.

One day, a sea turtle visited.

The frog invited him to see his magnificent home. The turtle tried to explain the vastness of the ocean—its depth, its breadth, its movement, its limitless horizon.

The frog could not comprehend it.

He had never known anything beyond the well.

The wife of wisdom understood the lesson: sometimes a person speaks with certainty not because they have seen the whole world, but because they have never stepped beyond the small one they know.

Sometimes the person who boasts the loudest has the smallest world.

She did not need to correct the frog. She did not need to prove the ocean existed. The ocean did not become smaller because someone could not imagine it.

And the blue house did not need to open every door simply because someone stood outside it.

The husband next door continued offering invitations. The wife next door continued smiling and waving. The wife of wisdom observed the exchange without surrendering her discernment. She could be courteous without becoming connected. She could wish another person well without inviting their energy into her home.

That was the work.

Not every invitation was an obligation. Not every familiarity was intimacy. And not every exchange was worth the price of entry.

The couple began to understand that energy, like attention, was something they could choose where to place. What they fed with their focus grew in their field. What they refused to entertain did not have to become a part of their story.

They did not argue with the wind.

They did not chase the whispers.

They returned to their work—to love, honor, respect, provide, and protect their family. Their sacred space was not a stage for proving anything. It was a home. A refuge. A place where their daughters could continue to bloom.

And so, the husband trimmed and tended the tree line. The wife returned to her meditation. Together, they honored the boundary they had planted—not as a declaration against anyone, but as a promise to the life within it.

Some boundaries are built from wood.

Others are built from a quiet decision: I will not intertwine my energy with what I have not invited into my life.

The wind still moved through the leaves. The whispers rose and fell. The couple heard what they heard, noticed what they noticed, and chose not to make every sound a summons.

There are lessons that arrive through noise. There are lessons that arrive through silence. And sometimes the wisest response is to let the wind carry what it came to carry—without carrying it yourself.

Then one winter, the street would grow quiet in a different way.
